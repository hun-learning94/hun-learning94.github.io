Hi there! I am a fourth-year Ph.D. candidate in Statistical Science at Duke University, advised by Simon Mak and Xiang Cheng.

I study how neural network architectures and their components drive model performance across tasks such as language reasoning, image and video generation, and tabular prediction. I first formulate the task and network, then use experiments, mechanistic analysis, and causal interventions to understand what shapes the network's capabilities. These insights guide architectural design and adaptation.

I am currently developing new sampling methods for language models, building on my work on uncertainty quantification for neural operators.

Before joining Duke, I earned an M.S. in Statistics and Data Science and a B.A. in Economics from Yonsei University. Contact me about research or collaboration.

  1. 1. Gyeonghun Kang, William Xingxu Chen, Xiang Cheng (2026). “How Do DiTs Denoise? An Aggregator–Predictor View of Attention and MLPs.” Preprint · arXiv:2610.29854.

    How attention and MLPs divide the work of denoising

    • DiTs perform local denoising: attention gathers relevant patches, while MLPs reconstruct clean patches from learned image representations. Attention reaches farther as noise increases, matching our theory.
    • Takeaway: For DiTs pretrained on diverse images, prioritize MLPs for domain adaptation. MLP memory can also be shared across layers, providing a common bank for memory augmentation.

  2. 2. Gyeonghun Kang*, Pengxi Liu*, Bingshuo Qian*, Xiang Cheng (2026). “The Noise-Transmission Burden of Diffusion Network: Why Image Prediction Can Outperform Noise Prediction.” Preprint · arXiv:2610.34834.

    Why image prediction can outperform noise prediction

    • Image, noise, and velocity prediction are not equivalent in finite networks. When model width is smaller than patch dimension, direct noise and velocity prediction incur an irreducible denoising-error floor. EDM preconditioning lowers this floor at high noise.
    • Takeaway: For high-dimensional image or video patches, make clean-data prediction the default, especially when model width is limited.

  3. 3. Gyeonghun Kang, Changwoo J. Lee, Xiang Cheng (2026). “Transformers Can Learn Posterior Predictive Distributions In-Context.” International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML).

    How network depth improves in-context Bayesian prediction

    • Transformers perform Bayesian prediction in a single forward pass: attention layers implement iterative gradient descent, and an MLP converts the result into a posterior predictive distribution.
    • Takeaway: For more accurate predictive distributions, give the model more inference steps: add attention layers or use test-time looping.
